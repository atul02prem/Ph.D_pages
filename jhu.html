Recommended (for Chellappa)

PhD in Electrical & Computer Engineering

  • "There are no lists of required courses." A minimum course load applies each semester.
  • Qualifying exam: question sets from 3 examiners (at least 2 tenure-track ECE), passed "before the beginning of the fourth semester". At most two attempts.
  • Research proposal (6 pages max) + Graduate Board Oral (GBO): 5-person committee, 2 hours, ideally before semester 6
  • Funded students "serve as a course/teaching assistant"
  • Public dissertation defense; typical limit of 7 years
  • PIs: Chellappa, Patel
Option with a CS advisor

PhD in Computer Science

  • 8 courses of 3 credits, at least 5 taught in CS, covering 4 of 5 areas: Applications, Reasoning, Software, Systems, Theory
  • B+ average across the 8; no grade below C-; done by end of year 2
  • Two qualifying projects with different faculty, each with a public report, by end of year 3
  • GBO (ideally year 3), one semester as a TA (+ EN.601.807 Teaching Practicum), CS Seminar in years 1–2
  • EN.500.603 Graduate Academic Ethics (first semester); AS.360.625 Responsible Conduct of Research (year 1)
  • PIs: Saria, Unberath, Yuille

Can your UNCW master's count? Partly. Up to 2 courses in CS; ECE doesn't say.

  • CS PhD: "Up to two graduate courses from other institutions may transfer with advisor approval. More require department approval." So your MS could cut the 8 courses to about 6.
  • ECE PhD: the catalogue doesn't address transfer. Since there's no required course list, your advisor sets how much coursework you take, so your MS mainly shows you already have the background.
  • Also: CS awards an MSE "along the way" after coursework + the first project. You don't need it, but it's there.
  • English test: the CS FAQ waives TOEFL/IELTS for applicants who "hold or will soon hold" a US master's. Your UNCW MS (Dec 2026) qualifies.

05If you enrolled in the D.Eng, would JHU find the employer?

No. The D.Eng is a research contract between your employer and JHU, so you bring the employer.

"The centerpiece of the Doctor of Engineering degree is a research collaboration between your employer and the Whiting School of Engineering."
"There is no way to fund a D.Eng student's program through JHU (payroll, etc.)"

You'd need a master's plus "significant professional experience" (3+ years recommended), a project your employer cares about, and a letter of support "indicating full financial support" from an employer co-advisor. You "cannot start" until funding and co-advising are secured. It's nonresidential, with conferences in Baltimore every January and June. Deadlines: Jan 1 (summer start), Jul 1 (spring start).

Not viable for you now. On an F-1 visa, a nonresidential, employer-funded doctorate doesn't fit. The PhD is the right route, and it's fully funded.

Can your UNCW master's count toward a CSU PhD? Very likely yes, as a lump sum.

CSU has two transfer routes. Only the first fits you:

  • Lump-sum credit for a completed master's (your route). Doctoral students "may request lump-sum credit from a previously awarded master's degree" on the Graduate School's GS3 form. The SE PhD allows up to 30 credits of "an applicable master's degree" toward depth, elective and dissertation credits. The CS PhD also allows up to 30, and the Computer Engineering PhD has a track built for students with an MS.
  • Individual courses. Courses must be graded B or better, taken within 10 years, at CSU's 500 level, and from a regionally accredited university. They also cannot have counted toward a previous degree, so your MS courses can't go this way. Only extra graduate courses outside your MS could.
  • UNCW qualifies: it is accredited by SACSCOC, one of the regional accreditors, and was reaffirmed in Dec 2023. Your MS will be awarded in Dec 2026, before the Fall 2027 start.
  • Not automatic: your advisor and the Graduate School must both approve, the program decides whether the MS is "applicable", and an official UNCW transcript must be on file. A decision comes within 30 days. You file after admission, through RAMweb.

If approved, about 42 credits remain at CSU for the SE PhD: SYSE 701, the dissertation, and a few courses (the "must-take" ones in the plan below).

05If you enrolled in the D.Eng, would CSU find the employer?

No. The sponsor has to come from your own employer, before you apply.

"Secure a practicum sponsor from within your company/organization. You must have a sponsor before your application will be considered."
"All D.Eng. applicants are expected to create a formal arrangement with an industry stakeholder."

The sponsor sends CSU a résumé and a signed commitment form, sits on your advisory committee, and must stay active for the whole program. CSU publishes no sponsor-matching service.

Also: on an F-1 visa you can't simply take a US industry job to create a sponsor. Fee-paying, part-time, employer-backed study is what the D.Eng is built around. It could make sense later, if you're working full-time in the US.

06Possible PIs and their recent work

The strongest angle: Daily + Gallegos on heavy-vehicle driver data

Daily (security) and Gallegos (human factors) publish together on ML and behavior using heavy-vehicle data: fuel efficiency from human and mechanical factors (TRR 2026), authenticating driver identity from vehicle data (2024), and driver responses to cyberattacks (2025). This is your Area 1 (messy real-world data, behavior), and it extends your TruckIDMe work directly. Pitch this side rather than firmware or reverse engineering.
